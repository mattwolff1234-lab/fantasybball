    How it works

    Points per game from here. This season's average, pulled towards last season's while the sample is small: after 12 games each counts half. Rookies, and players who barely played last season, lean on ESPN's preseason projection instead, which counts like only 3 games because it was a guess before he played.

    Injury bumps. When a player's minutes are up, the tool reads his ESPN news. If it says he is filling in for a hurt teammate, it counts how many of his games came without that teammate and how long the teammate is still out, and takes off the part of the jump that will not last. A one-game rest day barely moves it; a starter out for two months keeps most of it.

    Games left. His NBA team's games through the end of our fantasy playoffs, less the games the news says he will miss, times how often he played this season and last. A game he misses is not a zero: as in the draft guide, a free-agent pickup covers half of them.

    Uneven trades. Every roster is first topped up from free agents: if a free agent beats a team's worst player, the tool assumes the pickup happens anyway, so no trade gets credit for it. After that, the team that ends up with fewer players fills the open spot with the next best free agent, and the team with too many drops its weakest player. Then both rosters are played day by day for the rest of the season with only the starters' games counting, so a 2-for-1 is judged on what the extra body really adds.

    "Looks fair to them" means the other owner comes out ahead on ESPN's season averages, the numbers they will see. Positions are ignored, so check that your lineup still fills.
